# LV Ride Premium Business-Client & Corporate Account Operations Framework (Belgium, Founder-Operated)

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** May 13, 2026  
**Scope:** Antwerp-based founder-operated premium mobility operations focused on airport and business transport.

---

## 1) Business Operations Philosophy

### Core Operating Doctrine
- **Trust over scale:** every ride should increase confidence in LV Ride’s dependability and discretion.
- **Reliability over discounts:** service predictability and punctual execution are the value proposition, not price competition.
- **Professionalism over volume:** calm conduct, prepared execution, and business-grade communication supersede ride count.
- **Repeat relationship mentality:** optimize for the next 12 months of a client account, not a single transaction.
- **Calm premium culture:** no operational chaos, no urgency theater; structured updates and controlled escalation.
- **Accountability mindset:** every failure mode has an owner, a recovery action, and a post-ride learning loop.

### Founder Standard
As founder-operator/dispatcher/quality owner/first driver, maintain one non-negotiable principle:  
**“If it is not auditably reliable, it is not premium.”**

## 2) Ideal Early Business Customers

### Priority Early Segments (Antwerp & Airport Corridor)
1. **Small and medium businesses (SMBs)**
   - Teams with recurring office/client meeting transport needs.
   - Value: reliability, invoices, consistency.
2. **Hotels and boutique hospitality partners**
   - Front-desk bookings for premium guests.
   - Value: service quality that protects hotel reputation.
3. **Airport travelers (BRU/ANR corridor and business arrivals)**
   - High value from timing precision and proactive coordination.
4. **Consultants and executives**
   - Repeating routes and schedule-driven, low-friction expectation.
5. **Medical/professional appointment transport**
   - Time-sensitive, trust-driven, often recurring.
6. **Recurring private premium local mobility clients**
   - Predictable windows (daily/weekly), high relationship potential.

### Segments to Avoid in Founder Phase
- High-chaos on-demand mass-market demand pools.
- Low-margin ride contracts requiring discounting to win volume.
- Early-stage large enterprise tenders with heavy compliance overhead before core reliability metrics are stable.

## 3) Business Account Structure

### Account Tiering (Simple but Disciplined)
- **Tier A – Strategic Repeat (weekly frequency)**
- **Tier B – Recurring Business (2–8 rides/month)**
- **Tier C – Occasional Premium (ad hoc but business profile)**

### Account Data Model (Minimum Required)
For each business/VIP account:
- Legal and billing entity details.
- Primary and backup coordinators.
- Preferred communication channel (WhatsApp/SMS/email/phone).
- Regular pickup/dropoff locations.
- Preferred routes and buffer-time policies.
- Passenger-level preferences (silence, luggage handling, door protocol, call-before-arrival rule).
- Sensitive notes (discretion flags, privacy boundaries).
- Invoice cadence (weekly/biweekly/monthly) and PO/reference fields.
- Service recovery history.

### Relationship Continuity Controls
- Persistent account log after each ride (what worked, what to improve).
- Driver/dispatcher briefing before each recurring assignment.
- Quarterly account-review cadence for top accounts (founder-led).

## 4) Premium Business Customer Experience Standard

### Service Standardization
- **Punctuality discipline:** arrival buffer target by corridor type (e.g., airport pickups get stricter buffers).
- **Proactive communication:** short structured updates at accepted milestones.
- **ETA reliability:** avoid optimistic ETAs; communicate confidence ranges when risk exists.
- **Executive professionalism:** attire, clean vehicle, composed tone, no casual oversharing.
- **Airport coordination:** flight status monitoring + terminal/meeting-point confirmation.
- **Operational calmness:** no visible panic even under delay conditions.
- **Consistency:** same quality regardless of ride size/value.
- **Premium trust behavior:** discretion-first behavior during and after trip.

### Communication Milestones (Template)
1. Booking confirmed (with key details recap).
2. Pre-dispatch readiness confirmation.
3. Driver en route.
4. Arrived at pickup + exact pickup point descriptor.
5. Delay/risk alert (if needed) + recovery plan.
6. Trip completed + invoice reference if business account.

## 5) Business Booking Workflow

### A) Recurring Ride Workflow
1. Register recurring schedule and constraints.
2. Pre-validate route timing and realistic buffers.
3. Auto-generate future bookings with manual review gate.
4. Confirm upcoming block weekly with coordinator.
5. Lock assignment consistency where feasible.

### B) Scheduled Booking Workflow
1. Capture business account + traveler identity.
2. Confirm pickup/dropoff/flight or appointment metadata.
3. Apply account preferences automatically.
4. Dispatch readiness checkpoint at T-60 / T-30.
5. Execute milestone communications.

### C) Airport-Business Coordination Flow
- Flight monitoring trigger.
- Adjust pickup timing on delay/early arrival.
- Confirm terminal/meeting spot.
- Update client coordinator with revised ETA and confidence.

### D) Manual Override Procedures
Manual override allowed only for:
- SLA-risk prevention,
- safety/security reasons,
- client-approved itinerary change.

Every override must log:
- reason,
- decision owner,
- customer communication timestamp,
- recovery result.

### E) Priority Handling Discipline
Priority order during conflicts:
1. Active VIP/executive ride at risk.
2. Airport pickup with tight timing window.
3. Medical/professional time-critical appointment.
4. Recurring business account commitment.
5. Non-critical ad hoc request.

## 6) VIP / Executive Service SOP

### Etiquette and Conduct
- Address professionally (Mr./Ms./Title unless invited otherwise).
- Minimal conversation unless client initiates.
- Never discuss other clients, routes, or private details.
- Door and luggage assistance as default.

### Communication Tone
- Brief, neutral, confident, and respectful.
- No slang, no over-promising, no reactive defensiveness.

### Pickup Professionalism
- Be in position early with exact location clarity.
- Identify passenger discreetly.
- Confirm destination quietly and efficiently.

### Reliability & Delay Recovery
- If risk appears, inform early with factual impact.
- Offer concrete recovery path (re-route, revised ETA, alternate pickup point).
- Confirm customer acceptance.
- Post-trip apology and process correction where applicable.

### Trust Preservation Rules
- Discretion is permanent, not situational.
- Keep data exposure minimal (need-to-know only).
- Never compromise safety/legal compliance to “save face.”

## 7) Business-Control Tower Visibility

### Operational Views Required
- **Business booking priority board** (tiers + urgency).
- **Rides-at-risk panel** (ETA drift, flight changes, traffic incidents).
- **SLA monitor** (on-time status, comms SLA, resolution SLA).
- **Repeat-client context panel** (last 5 rides, preference exceptions, known risk windows).
- **Assignment consistency tracker** (who served which account, continuity gaps).

### Alerting Logic
Trigger proactive alerts when:
- ETA variance exceeds configured threshold.
- Driver dispatch not confirmed by checkpoint.
- Airport flight data changed materially.
- Communication milestone missed.

## 8) Operational SLA Philosophy

### Premium SLA Mindset (Realistic, Honest, Enforceable)
- Promise only what can be operationally controlled.
- Use corridor-specific punctuality targets (airport vs local business).
- Transparency beats perfection theater.
- Early warning is superior to late apology.
- Recovery speed and clarity are part of SLA quality.

### Suggested Pilot SLA Baseline
- **Pre-ride confirmation SLA:** 100% for scheduled business rides.
- **Milestone communication SLA:** 100% for VIP and airport rides.
- **On-time pickup target:** corridor-dependent target bands (e.g., >95% for recurring business under normal conditions).
- **Delay disclosure SLA:** immediate once risk exceeds threshold.
- **Incident postmortem SLA:** documented within 24 hours for material failures.

## 9) Business Pricing Strategy

### Pricing Principles
- Price for reliability and controlled execution.
- Build transparent premium corridors (airport, CBD/hotel/business nodes).
- Reward repeatability (predictable monthly volume), not bargain-seeking.
- Preserve margin needed for punctuality buffers and quality control.

### Model Components
- Base corridor rate + time-window adjustments.
- Airport coordination/service premium where applicable.
- Waiting-time and change policy clearly documented.
- Monthly invoice workflows for business accounts.
- Optional retainer/block-booking model for high-frequency accounts.

### Anti-Pattern Avoidance
- No race-to-the-bottom discounting.
- No unprofitable fixed-rate deals that degrade service quality.
- No hidden fees that undermine trust.

## 10) Business Repeatability Metrics

### Weekly/Monthly KPI Set
- Repeat booking rate (% rides from existing accounts).
- Airport vs business-local mix ratio.
- Business-client retention rate (30/60/90 day).
- Complaint frequency per 100 rides.
- SLA attainment (on-time, comms, resolution).
- Punctuality distribution (early/on-time/late bands).
- Recovery success rate (at-risk rides saved without cancellation).
- Referral generation rate from satisfied business clients.

### Quality Review Rhythm
- **Weekly founder ops review:** KPI trends + top incidents.
- **Monthly account review:** Tier A/B account health and expansion opportunities.
- **Quarterly service refinement:** adjust buffers, corridor pricing, and SOP based on data.

## 11) Founder Sustainability for Business Operations

### Capacity and Cadence Guardrails
- Set maximum rides/day and rides/week to protect reliability.
- Reserve fixed daily admin windows for dispatch and account management.
- Block pre-ride preparation time for VIP/airport segments.

### Workload Risk Controls
- Track overlap load: driving + dispatch + quality ownership.
- Define “red zone” thresholds (fatigue, delay backlog, missed comms).
- Introduce backup driver/dispatcher protocol before chronic overload appears.

### Sustainable Relationship Management
- Limit new-account onboarding pace to service stability.
- Prefer deepening existing accounts before broad acquisition.
- Use documented SOPs to reduce founder memory dependency.

### Founder Phase Exit Criteria (toward next stage)
Trigger first strategic hire when 2+ conditions persist for 4+ weeks:
- Utilization remains high and SLA risk rises.
- Missed non-driving control tasks (invoice/admin/QA).
- Repeat accounts request additional unsupported slots.
- Fatigue indicators threaten service consistency.
