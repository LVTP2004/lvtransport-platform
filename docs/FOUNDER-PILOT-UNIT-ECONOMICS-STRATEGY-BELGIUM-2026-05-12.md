# LV Ride Founder-Operated Premium Pilot Economics & Sustainability Strategy (Belgium)

_Date: 2026-05-12_

## Scope & Operating Assumptions

This framework is built for a **founder-operated premium pilot** in Belgium, focused on airport/business transfers with disciplined service radius, high reliability, and controlled growth.

Baseline assumptions used for realistic planning (adjust monthly with actuals):
- Service geography: Brussels + airport/business corridors, selectively extending only when profitable.
- Vehicle class: premium sedan/SUV equivalent expected by business and airport travelers.
- Operating model: founder as driver + dispatcher + quality owner during pilot.
- Ride volume: low-to-moderate by design, not growth-maximized.
- Positioning: reliability and trust premium, not lowest-price marketplace.

---

## 1) Founder-Pilot Economics Model

### A. Core pilot model
- **Economic unit = completed premium trip** with full cost attribution.
- **Margin discipline starts per ride**, not at monthly aggregate only.
- Founder’s manual dispatch replaces payroll expense but consumes finite energy/capacity; treat this as constrained production.

### B. Low-volume premium viability logic
- Accept lower absolute volume if:
  - average fare is premium,
  - dead mileage is controlled,
  - cancellation/idle time is constrained,
  - repeat bookings increase share of predictable demand.

### C. Suggested per-ride economics structure
For each ride track:
1. Gross booking value
2. Less payment fees
3. Less variable driving costs (energy + maintenance accrual + cleaning accrual)
4. Less deadhead allocation
5. Less SLA/recovery credits
= **Ride contribution margin**

Then monthly:
- Sum ride contribution margin
- Less fixed cost base (insurance, lease/depreciation, telecom, infra, admin/compliance)
= **Founder-pilot operating result**

### D. Quality-led economic edge
Founder-operated advantage in pilot:
- tighter service consistency,
- faster incident recovery,
- direct feedback loop from rider to operator,
- better premium retention and referral conversion.

## 2) Core Cost Categories

Use two layers: **fixed monthly base** and **variable per-ride/per-km**.

### Fixed costs (monthly)
- Vehicle lease/depreciation
- Insurance (commercial/passenger transport scope)
- Telecom/mobile data
- Core VPS/infrastructure baseline
- Software subscriptions and monitoring
- Compliance/admin/accounting
- Parking or base-location overhead where applicable

### Variable costs
- Fuel/charging
- Maintenance wear (tires, brakes, servicing reserve)
- Vehicle cleaning/prep turnover
- API/maps/realtime usage that scales with trip volume
- Payment processing fees
- Tolls/airport access fees (if not passed through)
- SLA recovery/compensation costs

### Cost control rule
- Keep fixed-cost base low enough that operation stays solvent at conservative utilization (not only best-case demand weeks).

---

## 3) Revenue Quality Strategy

Prioritize **revenue quality > ride count**.

### Priority hierarchy
1. Repeat premium riders
2. Airport transfer flows
3. Business account rides (SME, boutique corporate, executive assistant bookings)
4. High-trust referrals
5. One-off low-context rides (lowest priority)

### Revenue quality filters
- Favor prebooked, predictable, corridor-compatible trips.
- De-prioritize random requests that create large dead mileage or schedule volatility.
- Protect high-LTV customers with reliability guarantees and proactive communication.

---

## 4) Premium Pricing Philosophy

### Positioning principles
- Do **not** compete on lowest fare.
- Price on reliability, punctuality, experience, and accountability.
- Publish transparent fare logic for airport/business corridors.
- Avoid opportunistic surge spikes that undermine trust.

### Recommended fare architecture
- Base fare + distance/time components with corridor floors.
- Airport/business corridor reference prices with clear inclusions.
- Time-window premiums only when operationally justified and communicated upfront.
- VIP offerings limited to services you can reliably deliver (meet-and-greet, waiting buffer, luggage handling, priority support).

### SLA-backed premium claim
- If service guarantee is breached, predefined recovery protocol applies (credit/refund policy).
- Price premium is valid only when SLA discipline is consistently enforced.

---

## 5) Operational Margin Protection

### Guardrails
- Controlled service area with profitable boundaries.
- Explicit rejection rules for unprofitable long-tail requests.
- Dispatch sequencing that minimizes empty repositioning.
- Capacity throttling to prevent overload-driven service failures.
- Batching only where it does not degrade premium expectations.

### Time-window optimization
- Concentrate around airport/business peaks where premium willingness and predictability are highest.
- Avoid filling weak hours with discounted low-quality demand.

---

## 6) Founder Sustainability Constraints

### Realistic constraints
- Founder cannot simultaneously maximize driving hours, dispatch complexity, and quality control indefinitely.
- Fatigue degrades punctuality, communication quality, and safety posture.

### Practical operating cadence
- Target **5-8 premium rides/day** in steady state depending on average trip length and dispatch complexity.
- Treat **9+ rides/day** as occasional peak, not baseline expectation.
- Reserve daily admin + planning + recovery buffer (minimum 1.5-2.5 hours).
- Enforce at least 1 lighter day/week to prevent reliability decay.

---

## 7) Repeat-Customer Economics

### Why repeat matters
- Lower acquisition friction,
- lower coordination overhead,
- higher on-time matching efficiency,
- fewer disputes/cancellations,
- better route predictability and corridor density.

### Repeat economics model
- Track first-ride to second-ride conversion.
- Track 90-day repeat frequency by segment (airport, business, mixed).
- Track margin difference: repeat vs first-time riders.

### Business account retention value
- Small business account with consistent weekly airport rides can outperform many one-off retail bookings in margin stability.

---

## 8) Early KPI Economics

Track weekly and monthly with hard thresholds:
- Revenue per ride
- Contribution margin per ride
- Repeat booking %
- Airport/business ride ratio
- Dead mileage % of total km
- Cancellation rate + cancellation cost impact
- SLA incident rate + average recovery cost
- Utilization (paid-trip time / active operating time)
- Founder load index (driving time + dispatch/admin time)

Minimum viability signal:
- Positive contribution margin trend,
- rising repeat share,
- stable SLA performance,
- manageable founder load without quality deterioration.

---

## 9) Scaling Economics Evolution

### Stage 1 — Founder-operated premium pilot
- Validate corridor economics and SLA reliability.

### Stage 2 — Repeat-customer concentration
- Increase predictable demand density before adding fixed labor.

### Stage 3 — First small driver expansion
- Add one vetted driver only after process repeatability and utilization thresholds are met.

### Stage 4 — Operational optimization
- Improve scheduling logic, deadhead reduction, and KPI automation.

### Stage 5 — Dispatcher-assisted premium fleet
- Split founder role to preserve quality at higher ride volume.

### Stage 6 — Selective tooling/SaaS opportunities
- Explore operator tooling only if core mobility unit economics are already stable.

---

## 10) Biggest Economic Risks

- Underpricing in the name of growth
- Overexpansion beyond dispatch/control capacity
- Operational chaos from accepting poor-fit rides
- Founder burnout and service inconsistency
- Overbuilding infrastructure before demand maturity
- Low-quality customer mix (high support cost, low loyalty)
- Weak SLA discipline that erodes premium trust
- Excessive dead mileage and idle repositioning

---

## 11) What NOT to Optimize Too Early

- Vanity GMV and ride-count metrics
- Aggressive discount funnels
- Mass-market fare competition
- Premature multi-vehicle expansion
- Enterprise-grade infrastructure spend before need
- Paid acquisition at negative contribution margins
- Incentive programs that buy short-term volume but destroy pricing integrity

---

## 12) Realistic First-Year Operational Objectives

- Deliver stable, punctual premium service in chosen corridors.
- Build repeat-heavy rider mix with business and airport concentration.
- Mature KPI discipline and incident-response cadence.
- Maintain positive operating discipline with controlled cost base.
- Keep founder workload sustainable and quality-preserving.
- Preserve realtime/control-tower reliability as operational backbone.

## End-State Estimates (Pilot Planning View)

### Current operational sustainability estimate
- **Moderate and improving**: architecture/control readiness is strong, but sustainability depends on tight demand selection and disciplined daily capacity management.

### Strongest economic advantages currently
1. Founder-led service quality loop
2. Premium airport/business positioning
3. Improved realtime/control-tower foundation
4. Ability to enforce strict operational discipline early

### Biggest profitability risks
1. Underpricing relative to reliability commitment
2. Excessive dead mileage from loose service boundaries
3. Overloading founder capacity causing SLA failures
4. Accepting low-trust/low-fit demand mix

### Recommended early pricing discipline
- Set corridor-based premium floors and never price below contribution-margin safety threshold.
- Require transparent, predefined compensation rules for SLA misses.
- Review price floors monthly against fuel, insurance, maintenance, and cancellation actuals.

### Estimated sustainability after first 100 rides
- If repeat share reaches ~35-45% and dead mileage stays controlled, sustainability can move to **moderately robust pilot level** with improving predictability and lower per-ride coordination overhead.

### Realistic first-year operational scale
- **~1,000 to 1,800 rides/year** as a disciplined founder-operated premium range (not growth-maximized), depending on average trip length and availability windows.

### Readiness estimate for first additional driver
Consider adding first driver only when all are true for at least 8-12 continuous weeks:
- Consistent positive contribution margin after full cost allocation,
- stable SLA performance,
- repeat bookings >50% in core corridors,
- founder load persistently near sustainable ceiling,
- documented SOPs + quality checks ready for handoff.

### Viability estimate for sustainable founder-operated premium operations
- **Viable** if pricing integrity, corridor discipline, and founder cadence are protected.
- **Not viable** under discount-led volume pursuit or uncontrolled geographic expansion.
