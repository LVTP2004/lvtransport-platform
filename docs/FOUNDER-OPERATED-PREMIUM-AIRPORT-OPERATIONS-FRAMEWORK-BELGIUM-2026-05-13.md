# LV Ride Founder-Operated Premium Airport Operations Framework (Belgium)

Date: 2026-05-13  
Scope: Founder-operated pilot (operator + dispatcher + quality owner + first driver)  
Operating base: Antwerp, Belgium  
Primary objective: Make airport transfers LV Ride’s strongest and most trusted operational specialization.

---

## 1) Airport Operations Philosophy

### Core doctrine
- **Reliability over volume:** never accept demand that threatens on-time confidence for already-confirmed bookings.
- **Punctuality as premium value:** customer perception of luxury starts with timing certainty, not vehicle aesthetics.
- **Calm operational discipline:** run every airport trip from checklist discipline, not improvisation.
- **Airport professionalism mindset:** treat each transfer like an executive logistics mission with pre-brief, live monitoring, and post-run quality review.
- **Trust-loop mentality:** every touchpoint (confirmation, monitoring update, pickup, arrival) reduces uncertainty and builds repeat behavior.
- **Predictable premium service:** consistent process beats heroic effort; “boringly reliable” is the premium standard.

### Operating principles for founder mode
- Operate at a controlled utilization rate (target 65–75%) to preserve buffer capacity.
- Keep one single source of truth in control tower for status, ETA, risks, and next actions.
- Protect service integrity by refusing low-fit jobs (tight timing, underpriced long waits, unclear pickup instructions).
- Maintain short operational retrospectives daily and weekly to tighten SOP precision.

## 3) Flight Monitoring Operational Model

### Flight-awareness philosophy
- Flight number is mandatory for all airport pickups.
- Monitoring begins at booking confirmation and intensifies within 6 hours pre-landing.
- Treat scheduled arrival as a baseline, not truth.

### Monitoring cadence (founder-operable)
- **T-24h:** confirm booking data completeness (flight, terminal expectations, passenger contact).
- **T-6h:** first operational check; classify risk (green/amber/red) based on delays and traffic forecast.
- **T-2h:** second check; pre-select departure window and fallback buffer.
- **T-60m to touchdown:** active watch window with 15–20 minute checks or live feed.
- **Post-landing:** switch from flight status to curbside pickup orchestration.

### Delay and uncertainty handling
- Build **two ETA layers**:
  - flight-adjusted touchdown ETA
  - passenger-ready-for-pickup ETA (immigration + baggage + walking)
- Define uncertainty ranges:
  - low: ±10 min
  - medium: ±20 min
  - high: ±35+ min
- Trigger proactive reassurance messages when uncertainty crosses medium threshold.

### Customer reassurance communication standard
- Keep messages short, calm, and specific: status + what LV Ride is doing + next check-in time.
- Never ask customer to “figure out” airport logistics alone; always give the next action.

## 4) Airport Pickup SOP

### Contact timing protocol
- **Pre-arrival message (before landing):** introduce pickup process and where updates will come from.
- **Touchdown acknowledgment:** confirm driver readiness and pickup sequencing.
- **Baggage-phase check-in:** align expected exit door/meeting zone.
- **Final coordination message/call:** precise pickup point with landmark-based instructions.

### Arrival coordination and pickup-point discipline
- Use airport-specific predefined pickup templates (terminal, level, zone, landmark).
- Confirm passenger descriptor (name + luggage count) and vehicle descriptor before meeting.
- If pickup area congestion rises, immediately trigger alternate point script.

### Terminal awareness discipline
- Maintain a concise BRU/CRL/AMS/EIN quick-reference sheet in control tower:
  - arrival hall logic
  - short-stay vs pickup lane options
  - walking-time estimates from baggage claim to curb

### Luggage professionalism
- Driver exits vehicle when feasible, assists with luggage handling, and confirms fragile/high-value items placement.
- Confirm destination and preferred comfort settings (temperature, silence/work mode).

### No-show handling SOP
- **+10 min after agreed meet time:** call + message.
- **+20 min:** second call + explicit wait policy reminder.
- **+30 min:** escalate to no-show workflow in control tower, log evidence, and close with professional follow-up.

### Delayed-arrival recovery SOP
- If flight delay >45 min from initial baseline, provide revised pickup window and reassurance.
- If delay threatens next commitment, trigger pre-defined recovery playbook (re-slot, decline new leads, arrange backup partner if policy allows).

## 5) Airport Timing Discipline

### Departure buffer framework (initial pilot)
- **Antwerp → BRU:** baseline transit + 30–45 min protection buffer.
- **Antwerp → CRL:** baseline transit + 45–60 min buffer.
- **Antwerp → AMS/EIN:** baseline transit + 45–75 min buffer (time-of-day dependent).
- Always include parking/walk transfer time at airport in final customer planning ETA.

### Traffic-risk philosophy
- Timing decisions use risk bands, not average route duration.
- Any corridor with incident risk, peak-hour compression, or weather stress automatically shifts to conservative departure profile.

### Lateness prevention mindset
- “Too early is recoverable; late is reputational damage.”
- Do not stack bookings if worst-case travel overlap is plausible.

### Operational redundancy
- Maintain at least one contingency option per trip:
  - alternate route
  - alternate pickup point
  - alternate departure threshold

### Airport wait-time handling
- Define included wait windows by segment (e.g., standard, executive, VIP handling).
- At booking time, set transparent wait policy and extension rates.

### Controlled scheduling density
- For founder-operated mode, cap to **1 airport-critical ride per active risk window** unless both rides are in low-volatility time blocks.
- Preserve recovery space between assignments (minimum 45–60 minutes for airport-to-airport sequences).

## 6) Premium Airport Customer Experience

### Calm communication standard
- Neutral, confident tone.
- No operational panic language.
- Every message includes status clarity and next step.

### Proactive update protocol
- Send updates at key state changes (en route, monitoring delay, landed, ready, met, departed airport).
- Default to proactive messaging before customer asks.

### Reassurance behavior
- Acknowledge uncertainty explicitly and attach a control statement (“I’m monitoring your flight and will update again at [time]”).

### Professional greeting standard
- Greeting includes customer name, courteous welcome, immediate luggage support, and smooth transition to vehicle.

### Executive/business etiquette
- Offer “work-friendly ride mode” (quiet cabin, charging availability, minimal interruption).
- Confirm route preference where relevant (fastest vs smoothest).
- Maintain strict discretion on passenger identity and trip purpose.

### Repeat-customer relationship building
- Keep preference memory notes (temperature, conversation preference, route style, assistance needs).
- After successful trip, send concise thank-you and rebooking ease cue.
- Prioritize high-trust repeat clients in peak slot allocation.

### Premium consistency expectation
- Define non-negotiables:
  - punctual departure discipline
  - clean vehicle standard
  - predictable communication cadence
  - respectful professional behavior

## 7) Airport Control Tower Visibility

### Live airport ride visibility model
Track each airport booking with a standardized lifecycle board:
1. Booked
2. Pre-validated
3. Flight monitored
4. Driver pre-positioning
5. Landed / passenger processing
6. Passenger met
7. Departed airport
8. Completed + QA logged

### Rides-at-risk monitoring
- Flag any ride with:
  - ETA deviation above threshold
  - unresolved passenger contact
  - airport congestion anomalies
  - overlapping commitment risk

### Delayed-flight awareness
- Delay feed should surface both current delay magnitude and delay trend (stable vs increasing).

### ETA risk indicators
- Use simple color codification:
  - Green: on track
  - Amber: manageable risk
  - Red: SLA risk / intervention required

### Operational timeline tracking
- Log time-stamped events for every critical transition.
- Maintain quick post-ride incident tags for quality analytics.

### Reassignment readiness (future-safe even if founder is sole driver today)
- Keep driver handover SOP template ready in system.
- Define minimum data packet for reassignment: booking ID, passenger profile, terminal/pickup details, live ETA risk, special instructions.

## 8) Airport Incident Recovery

### Incident taxonomy
- Flight delay/cancellation
- Traffic disruption
- Customer no-show/late emergence
- Pickup-point access restriction
- Vehicle issue
- Communication outage

### Recovery doctrine
- **Stabilize → Communicate → Recover → Document → Learn**

### Incident playbooks (founder-usable)
- **Delay/cancellation:** provide revised plan options within 10 minutes of confirmation.
- **Traffic disruption:** disclose ETA impact early; activate alternate route or revised pickup sequencing.
- **No-show:** follow timed no-show ladder with evidence logs.
- **Vehicle issue:** immediately communicate replacement/alternative and realistic revised ETA.

### Communication SLA during incidents
- First customer update within 5–10 minutes of incident awareness.
- Next checkpoint promised with explicit timestamp.

### Post-incident trust recovery
- After resolution, send concise accountability message with what was done.
- When appropriate, apply goodwill gesture policy (e.g., priority rebooking, courtesy adjustment).

### Learning loop
- Every incident logged with root cause, containment action, recovery outcome, and SOP adjustment decision.
- Weekly founder review identifies top 3 preventable risk patterns and preventive controls.
